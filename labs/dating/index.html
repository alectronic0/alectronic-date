How ratings work

Each rating comes from a published rule applied to sourced facts. Every card shows its full track record with links, so you can judge for yourself.

  • ⛔ Avoid A regulator or court took action over fake or staff-run profiles, or found the service deceived customers.
  • ⚠️ Caution A serious fine, lawsuit, data exposure or investigation in the last years; or members aren't verified; or it charges per message.
  • 🔍 AI-checked: no red flags An AI agent searched regulator, court and press sources and found nothing serious on record recently, and members can be verified. It is not a personal recommendation.

A rating can be set by hand only with a written reason, and the card then says "by hand". App Store and Trustpilot scores are shown for context but never decide a rating: almost every big app scores 4+ on the App Store, and Trustpilot reviews skew towards complaints.

Spotting a romance scam

  • They want to move off the app to WhatsApp or Telegram straight away.
  • They always have a reason not to video call or meet.
  • The conversation turns to money: an emergency, a travel cost, a "sure thing" crypto or trading investment.
  • They ask for gift cards, bank transfers or access to your accounts.
  • They ask you to keep the relationship secret from friends and family.
  • Their photos turn up under other names in a reverse image search.

Where to report

  • Lost money? Call your bank first, then Action Fraud on 0300 123 2040. In Scotland, call Police Scotland on 101.
  • A profile or message on an app: use the app's own report button so the account can be banned.
  • An app not dealing with illegal content: the Online Safety Act requires dating services to act on it, and Ofcom enforces this.
